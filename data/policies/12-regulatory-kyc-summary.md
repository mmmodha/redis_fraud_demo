# Regulatory Summary — Know Your Customer (KYC)

**Document type:** Regulatory guidance summary (synthetic, for training)
**Disclaimer:** This is an internal training summary written for the
demo. It is not a substitute for the bank's official KYC manual or for
external counsel.

## Purpose of KYC

KYC controls exist to ensure the bank knows the identity of every
customer it onboards, the source of their funds, and the expected
pattern of their account activity. They make the bank's other fraud and
anti-financial-crime controls meaningful — without identity verification,
risk models cannot attribute behaviour reliably to a real person.

## Levels of KYC

The bank operates a three-level KYC scheme.

- **Level 0 — pre-customer.** No card issued. Marketing data only.
- **Level 1 — basic verification.** Government ID + selfie liveness +
  proof of address. Sufficient to issue a debit card with a USD 5,000
  monthly authorised limit.
- **Level 2 — enhanced verification.** Level 1 + source-of-funds
  declaration + sanction-screening clearance + ongoing transaction
  monitoring enrolment. Required for credit cards, premium products, and
  any account that breaches USD 10,000 in a calendar month.

## Triggers for re-verification

A customer must complete fresh KYC checks when any of the following
occur:

- 24 months since last documented verification.
- Material change in declared occupation or source of funds.
- Move to a higher-risk country of residence per the bank's country-risk
  matrix.
- Authorised activity exceeds 3× the customer's declared expected
  monthly volume for two consecutive months.

## Interaction with fraud controls

A new account that has not yet reached Level 2 stays on new-account
fraud defaults indefinitely (see new-account guidance). Fraud operators
may not relax thresholds for a Level 1 account beyond the published
new-account defaults — that decision requires KYC uplift first.
